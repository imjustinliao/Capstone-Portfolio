Overall Capstone Andriy Demyanyuk

Why This Project Mattered to Me

I signed up for this capstone because I wanted to work on something that felt bigger than a normal homework assignment. Serene stood out because the whole idea was about helping people meet in person instead of staying in endless chats and swipes. That goal mattered to me personally. It did not feel like checking a box for a grade. It felt like trying a different take on a space I already thought about as a user.

The project also gave me room to take backend work more seriously than I had in shorter classes. I knew I would own a side of the product for a whole quarter, not just finish a lab and move on. That responsibility was part of what made the quarter feel meaningful, even when I was stuck on something I did not know how to fix yet.

Learning How to Build a Larger System

Working on one project for many weeks was different from the usual pattern of smaller assignments that start and end in isolation. I had to keep the whole picture in my head while still only building one piece at a time. Breaking the work into chunks helped me keep going, but it also showed me how easy it is to assume two parts fit together until you actually connect them and something breaks.

I started to notice more how one decision can affect another part of the system. A larger project needs more planning upfront than I am used to, and more checking when I change something partway through. I am not saying I planned everything perfectly, but I got better at asking what else might shift when I change one area. I had heard about systems thinking in class before, but this was the first time I really felt it on a project this size.

Working Through Unclear Problems

A lot of what I hit this quarter did not come with a neat answer in the textbook. Location-based matching in particular was unclear at first. I had to figure out what should happen when signals conflict, when timing is off, or when the behavior I wanted only showed up once the app and my side were both hooked up. Integration was like that too. Something could work on my side and still fail once Justin’s screens talked to my work.

I got more comfortable moving in small steps when I was lost. I would reproduce the issue, read whatever error I had, try a change, and repeat. Switching between tools felt less overwhelming once I accepted that debugging often means following the same problem through several places. I used to want a clear plan before I started. This project pushed me to start anyway and fix things as I went.

Building in a Team Instead of Alone

Justin was building the iOS side while I worked on the server side of things. For a while we could each make progress in parallel, but we still had to agree on what we were building toward so we did not drift. As the quarter went on, the two halves depended on each other more. A small change in how a screen worked or what data it expected could mean I had to adjust something on my end, and the same went the other way. We checked in on a regular rhythm, and those check-ins mattered more when integration picked up.

The pace of the work felt steadier when we said clearly what we had changed and what we still assumed. When we waited too long to compare notes, we ended up confused and had to redo work. By the end I understood better that building with someone else is not only about splitting tasks. We also have to stay aligned on what we are building so the frontend and backend still match when we put them together.

What I Took Away from the Quarter

I learned something about how I handle long, open-ended work. I can stay with a problem longer than I thought if I break it down and treat confusion as normal for a week or two, not as a sign that I picked the wrong project. Backend work also feels less abstract to me now. It is not only about making an endpoint return the right thing in a demo. It is about how that behavior fits with the rest of the product while someone else is changing their side too.

Isolated assignments let me finish and forget. This one stayed with me because the same codebase kept growing and Justin kept depending on what I shipped. Uncertainty, collaboration, and a longer timeline changed how I think about what done means. I am more aware of my own habits, like when I rush versus when I slow down and check my work, and I want to keep that awareness when I work on whatever comes next.

Conclusion

This capstone asked more of me than a shorter class usually does. I spent the quarter on one big effort, dealing with messy problems, and coordinating with Justin so our work still matched. It was stressful at times and it felt good when things finally lined up.

If we keep going with the app, there is still plenty to tighten and improve, but I am not laying out a full roadmap here. What I am taking away is the habit of thinking across the whole system, talking often when someone else depends on my choices, and staying patient when the answer is not obvious on the first try. That matters more to me than any single feature we finished this quarter.
