Annotated Bibliography

I grouped the resources below by theme to show how I learned the tools and methods used while building Serene. Each theme has a short annotation and APA-style entries for the sources I drew on.

Backend APIs and Supabase

We used Flask for the REST API and Supabase (Postgres + auth) for persistence. I relied on official docs and REST/API design sources to align request/response shapes with the iOS client and to structure endpoints for discovery, matching, and meet lifecycle.

  • Fielding, R. T. (2000). Architectural styles and the design of network-based software architectures (Doctoral dissertation). University of California, Irvine.
  • Pallets. (n.d.). Flask documentation. https://flask.palletsprojects.com/ [Verify details]
  • Supabase. (n.d.). Supabase documentation. https://supabase.com/docs [Verify details]
  • PostgreSQL Global Development Group. (n.d.). PostgreSQL documentation. https://www.postgresql.org/docs/ [Verify details]

iOS Development and SwiftUI

I built the Serene iOS app in SwiftUI and followed Apple’s documentation and HIG for navigation, state, and MapKit integration. These sources guided architecture choices and permission flows (location, notifications).

  • Apple Inc. (n.d.). SwiftUI. Apple Developer Documentation. https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui [Verify details]
  • Apple Inc. (n.d.). Human Interface Guidelines. Apple Developer. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines [Verify details]
  • Apple Inc. (n.d.). MapKit. Apple Developer Documentation. https://developer.apple.com/documentation/mapkit [Verify details]
  • Apple Inc. (n.d.). Core Location. Apple Developer Documentation. https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation [Verify details]
  • Hudson, P. (2020). SwiftUI by Example. Hacking with Swift. [Verify details]

Location, Maps, and Geospatial Computation

We used Haversine distance for “who’s nearby” and Apple MapKit for in-app maps. I referenced geospatial and mapping docs to implement and tune the proximity logic and to handle location permissions and privacy (e.g., no persistent precise location).

  • Apple Inc. (n.d.). Displaying maps with MapKit. Apple Developer Documentation. https://developer.apple.com/documentation/mapkit [Verify details]
  • Simott, R. W. (1984). Virtues of the Haversine. Sky and Telescope, 68(2), 159. [Verify details]
  • Wikipedia. (n.d.). Haversine formula. In Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haversine_formula [Verify details]
  • MDN Web Docs. (n.d.). Geolocation API. https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Geolocation_API [Verify details]

Safety, Privacy, and Trust Design

We designed for bounded meetups (30-minute window, QR check-in), optional location sharing with trusted contacts, and real photo capture. I used safety and trust-design sources to shape the flow without over-claiming what the app could guarantee.

  • Cavoukian, A. (2009). Privacy by design: The 7 foundational principles. Information and Privacy Commissioner of Ontario. [Verify details]
  • Hancock, J. T., & Toma, C. (2009). Putting your best face forward: The accuracy of online dating photographs. Journal of Communication, 59(2), 367–386.
  • Finkel, E. J., Eastwick, P. W., Karney, B. R., Reis, H. T., & Sprecher, S. (2012). Online dating: A critical analysis from the perspective of psychological science. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 13(1), 3–66.
  • Nielsen Norman Group. (n.d.). Trust and credibility. https://www.nngroup.com/topic/trust/ [Verify details]

UX Research and Product Strategy

I used UX research and product strategy sources to scope the problem (college dating, authenticity, IRL meetups), run lightweight research, and prioritize MVP features. These informed the Polaroid-first onboarding and “meet IRL” as the north star.

  • Nielsen, J. (2012). Usability 101: Introduction to usability. Nielsen Norman Group. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-101-introduction-to-usability/
  • Goodman, E., Kuniavsky, M., & Moed, A. (2012). Observing the user experience: A practitioner’s guide to user research (2nd ed.). Morgan Kaufmann. [Verify details]
  • Norman, D. (2013). The design of everyday things (Revised and expanded ed.). Basic Books.
  • Krug, S. (2014). Don’t make me think, revisited: A common sense approach to web usability (3rd ed.). New Riders.
  • IDEO. (n.d.). Design Kit: Methods. https://www.designkit.org/methods [Verify details]
