Overall Capstone Justin Liao

Why I Cared About This Project

I have felt for a while that many dating apps are built more around swiping and matching than actually helping people meet. The apps keep people engaged, but a lot of conversations never turn into anything real. That stood out to me because most people say they want real connection, not just more time in the app.

That is why Serene felt meaningful to me. I liked the idea of working on something that focused more on meeting in real life. It also matched what I care about in product and design. I like thinking about how people actually behave, what makes them hesitate, and what makes an experience feel real instead of forced. That was a big reason I stayed interested in the project.

Learning to Turn an Idea into a Real Product

At the beginning, I mostly had a direction and some interface ideas. Turning that into actual screens, flows, and decisions was much harder than I expected. Once I had to design every step and then build it, the idea became much more concrete. I had to think carefully about what happened first, what could wait, and how onboarding, discovery, and the map flow all connected.

Breaking the product into smaller parts helped me move forward, but it also showed me how much I was still figuring out as I went. Some screens looked fine in Figma, but once I started building them, I could see problems more clearly. Sometimes one design choice created extra work somewhere else. That made me realize that design and implementation are much more connected than I used to think.

Getting Comfortable with iOS Development

This project was also one of my first real experiences working seriously in iOS development. At first, Xcode felt confusing, and I was not used to how everything was organized. I would lose track of where problems were coming from, and even simple things sometimes took longer than I expected. MapKit, state management in SwiftUI, and trying to use liquid glass without hurting readability all took time for me to understand.

Over the quarter, I became more comfortable with not knowing everything right away. I stopped expecting the first version to work well and started seeing revision as part of the process. That helped me stay calmer when something was confusing. I usually like moving fast, but this project made me slow down more, read documentation more carefully, and work through problems step by step.

Building with Another Person, Not by Myself

Andriy was working on the backend while I focused on product, design, and the iOS frontend. We had a weekly call to stay aligned, and once we started connecting the frontend and backend, we had more calls because small issues started showing up more often. Sometimes it was a request format, sometimes a field name, and sometimes it was just that we were assuming different things. Those moments made the project feel much more real.

There were also times when fixing one issue meant both of us had to make changes. That helped me see that frontend work is not separate from the rest of the system. If I changed a flow or assumed certain data would be there, that affected someone else’s work too. By the end of the quarter, I understood much better how important it is to communicate early, not just when something has already gone wrong.

What Changed for Me

My thinking changed gradually during the quarter. Before this project, I often treated design and engineering as two separate stages. With Serene, I started to see them as part of the same process. A design choice affected implementation, and implementation limits affected design. That changed the way I think about building products.

I also learned something about myself. At the start, unfamiliar tools felt intimidating. By the end, they felt more normal because I had spent enough time working through them. I realized I can handle confusion better than I thought, as long as I keep moving and focus on the next step. This project was not just about learning SwiftUI or MapKit. It also taught me how I work when I care about something but do not fully know what I am doing yet.

Conclusion

This capstone was the first time I had to handle product direction, UI/UX, and a mobile frontend together over a full quarter. It was not always smooth, but it helped me understand my own process much better. I learned how I respond when things are unclear, how I work with another person on a shared system, and how much patience real product work takes.

If we continue building Serene, I would want to improve onboarding and make the frontend and backend feel even more connected. More than anything, though, I am taking away the habit of breaking big ideas into smaller steps, checking assumptions early, and staying with the work long enough for it to start making sense.
