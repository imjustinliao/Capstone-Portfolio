Overall Capstone Justin Liao

Starting Point

I came into the capstone wanting to own product direction and the user-facing experience. Our team chose to build Serene, a dating app for college students that pushes toward meeting in real life instead of endless swiping. I took the lead on product, design, and frontend. Andriy owned the backend and systems. From the start we agreed on a tight scope: real profiles with Polaroids, location-based discovery, and one IRL meet with safety guardrails. That framing let me focus on the flow users would actually see and touch.

Turning Research into Decisions

We ran stakeholder interviews and surveys and looked at apps like Hinge and Coffee Meets Bagel. What stuck with me was how often conversations faded and how much people overthought their profiles. Apps were built for engagement, not for meeting in real life. I used that to argue for Polaroid-first onboarding and meet IRL as the north star. We positioned for college students 18 to 26 and bet on proximity-based discovery instead of copying swipe mechanics. The research gave me a clear basis to cut scope and say no to deeper in-app chat so we could ship one coherent story.

Building the Frontend Under Constraints

I led the Figma design and then the SwiftUI build and Apple Maps integration. The main constraint was time: we had to ship an iOS app that could talk to Andriy’s API and still feel focused. I kept the flow in short steps so it never felt overwhelming and gave clear rationale for location and notifications. Early wireframes had too many steps. I collapsed them into a single sequence: show once, then capture. In SwiftUI I structured navigation and state around discovery, profile, and meet flow. When the meet flow gained states like pending, confirmed, and expired, I refactored so the frontend could stay in sync with the backend without guesswork.

Collaboration and Coordination

Andriy and I coordinated weekly. We mapped the full user flow early so design and backend could stay aligned. He used that flow to define API endpoints and safety checks. I wired the app to his backend near the end of the quarter. Aligning on request and response shapes and on safety behavior up front meant we did not have to rework the client when his API changed. I learned that spending time on the handoff pays off. We did not merge frontend and backend until we had a clear checklist, and that kept integration predictable.

Testing and Iteration

We shipped to beta testers via TestFlight and ran through a checklist for auth, discovery, match, meet, and safety. Testers said the Polaroid and meet flow felt different from typical apps. The confusion we heard was about when to enable location and what nearby meant. We adjusted copy and one onboarding step in response. I had to let go of how I thought the flow should be explained and follow what actually confused people. We did not collect formal metrics. The iteration was qualitative: we changed what testers pointed at and documented why.

What I Learned

Doing the full loop from research to scope to design to implementation to integration to beta taught me to make decisions under constraints. I learned to use research to justify cuts and to iterate from feedback instead of assumptions. Aligning with Andriy on the API and safety early prevented rework later. I also learned that collapsing the onboarding from many steps to a single sequence improved clarity. The main takeaway for me is that a small set of clear decisions, shared with the backend owner and tested with real users, beats a long feature list that never ships.

Conclusion

Serene was a group capstone. I led product, design, and frontend. Andriy led backend and systems. Together we shipped an iOS app that keeps the digital part lightweight and pushes people toward safe, in-person meets when there is intent on both sides. I take away a clearer sense of how to run research into decisions, build a frontend under constraints, and coordinate with a backend owner so the integration stays predictable. The artifacts we left are the log of that progression.
