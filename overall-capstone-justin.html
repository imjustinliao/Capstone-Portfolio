Overall Capstone Justin Liao

Introduction

For my capstone project, I worked on product direction, UI UX design, and the iOS frontend for Serene. Serene is a dating app for college students that tries to move dating from endless swiping and messaging to meeting in real life through serendipity.

This project also pushed me into iOS development for the first time. I worked with my co-founder, Andriy, who built the backend. Building the app in parallel and keeping the frontend and backend aligned made this feel closer to real software work than most classes.

Project Motivation and Technology Choices

My starting belief was that most dating apps are built for dopamine boosts. People swipe a lot, overthink profiles, and chats lose momentum. Serene takes the opposite approach. We keep the digital part lightweight, then push people toward safe, meaningful in-person interaction when there is intent on both sides.

For the frontend, I chose SwiftUI because it is the modern iOS way to build interfaces and it helped me move fast once I understood the patterns. I used Xcode as the main IDE and Apple Maps with MapKit because the product depends on location-based discovery. I designed the user flows in Figma first, then translated them into SwiftUI screens with a minimal black and white style so the product felt clear and not cluttered.

System Design and Development Process

To make the work manageable, I broke the app into core flows and built them one at a time. I started with the basic navigation and page structure, then added the onboarding flow, and then the map-based discovery flow. I also tried to keep UI components reusable so the design stayed consistent across screens.

Because the backend was being built separately, I stayed focused on making the frontend easy to connect later. I wrote clear comments in important places, kept the data models simple, and planned the API wiring so we could integrate without rewriting large parts of the UI.

Challenges and Collaboration

The hardest technical parts for me were MapKit, state management in SwiftUI, and implementing the liquid glass style without breaking readability. I was also new to Xcode, so the IDE and the iOS project structure felt confusing at first. A lot of the early time went into reading developer documentation, following tutorials, and learning how SwiftUI wants state to flow through views.

Collaboration was also a big part of the work. Since Andriy and I worked on different parts of the system, we had a weekly sync call to stay aligned. When we started connecting the frontend and backend, we had additional calls to resolve bugs and adjust small details like request formats and data fields. Getting those handoffs right taught me how much communication matters when one side depends on the other.

Conclusion and Future Work

Overall, building the frontend for Serene helped me learn how to take a product idea and turn it into a working iOS prototype. I also learned how different iOS development feels compared to web development, especially around state, navigation, and debugging inside Xcode.

If we continue this project, the next work would focus on polishing the onboarding clarity, tightening the frontend and backend integration, and running small campus tests to see whether students understand the main idea quickly. The most important lesson for me is that design and engineering have to stay connected, especially when the goal is to get people to meet in real life instead of spending time inside the app.
